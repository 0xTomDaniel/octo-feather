Judgment questions: labelled, benched, advisory

docs / specs / judgment.spec.html

Some questions the factory asks are about meaning, so no check can answer them from facts (ADR 0008). Each one is put to a classifier model as one closed question, and its answer counts only as far as real labelled history shows the model gets it right.

User stories

As the director, every judgment question comes with real cases I checked, each with its label, its reason and its source, so the owner can review any label later and overturn it.

As the owner, each judgment question's model is chosen by its measured accuracy, calibration and cost on cases it was not tuned on, not by feel.

As the owner, a model's judgment shown beside a review is labelled as one, and nothing blocks on it until its question is calibrated.

Acceptance criteria

Given a maintainer with the kit's judgment questions, When they open one question's labelled set, Then it shows one closed question, its fixed labels, and at least fifteen cases from the factory's real history, each with its label, a one-line reason and its source, with a held-out share marked apart.

Given a maintainer with a judgment question's labelled set, When they run the factory evals' bench for that question, Then every available classifier shows its accuracy, its calibration and its cost on that question, the held-out score shown apart, and one model is named as the question's choice.

Given a supervisor whose lane head the code reviewer judged, and a judgment question whose held-out accuracy and calibration are below its accepted threshold, When the question's chosen model answers it beside that review, Then the merge ask shows the answer labelled as a model judgment beside the code reviewer's verdict, and the merge ask is ready without it.

Modular boundaries

A question set owns one judgment question: its fixed wording, its labels, and its labelled cases with reason, source and split. Caller-facing seam: the set's data, read by the bench and by the advisory judge. Dependency direction: self-contained; it depends on no model. Observable scope: the question, its labels, and each case's label, reason, source and split.

The bench owns measuring every available classifier on each question set. Caller-facing seam: the factory evals (quality#boundary-evals). Dependency direction: external-provider; it depends on classifier models through Pi and on the question sets. Observable scope: per question and model, accuracy, calibration and cost, held-out apart, and the chosen model.

The advisory judge owns answering one question with its chosen model beside a lane's review. Caller-facing seam: its labelled answer in the merge ask. Dependency direction: external-provider; it depends on the chosen classifier through Pi and on the question set. Observable scope: the answer, its confidence, and the label that marks it a model judgment.

Rules

A judgment question sits on the ladder rung ADR 0008 names for judgment; this spec does not restate that rung.

Each question has a closed answer set: the model picks one of its fixed labels, with a confidence.

The list of questions is open, and adding one is factory QA: a lane or a corrector proposes it with its labelled set, the director checks and owns the labels, the owner reviews them later, the factory evals bench it, and it stays advisory until it meets its threshold.

Labels come from the factory's real history; a lane drafts them, the director checks and owns them, and the owner reviews them later, with nothing waiting on that review.

A label the owner overturns changes the set, and the question's bench and choice are rerun.

Held-out cases never shape a question's wording or its model choice; they give the score its threshold is judged on.

Calibration means a model's confidence tracks how often it is right on that question.

A judge is advisory until its question's held-out accuracy and calibration meet a threshold the lane proposes and the director accepts; until then no gate reads its answer.
